Scorigami: Test Cricket (Men’s)

A scorigami is a score that has never happened before. Originally coined for the NFL, the term has since been taken up by fans of other sports. This is the Test cricket version, in two parts: the final run total of every men’s Test innings and how it ended, and the margin every men’s Test has been won by.

What the colours mean

Each square is a team total from 0 to 999, coloured by how innings on that total have ended.

  • Complete – all out. A side has been bowled out for exactly this total at least once.
  • Complete – not out. Innings have ended on this total, but none of them all out. They were declared, won the match by chasing down a target, or were still batting when the match was drawn.
  • Scorigami. No men’s Test innings has ever finished on this total.

Under Display, the frequency heat map shades every total in blue by how often it has come up (darker means more often; the Grids view also shows the count), with a dot marking totals that have never ended all out. High-contrast colours turn the scorigamis orange. With filters on, every view recolours using only the innings that match.

How endings are decided

  • All out: the batting side was bowled out.
    • Short-handed: the innings ended with fewer than ten wickets down because a batter was absent hurt, retired hurt or ill and didn’t come back. A few scorecards record a batter who retired hurt as “retired not out”. Those count as retired hurt here and are marked with an asterisk (*), with a note saying where they were recorded that way.
  • Declared: the batting side closed its innings.
    • Forfeited*: counted as a declaration on 0. The only men’s Test forfeits came at Centurion in 2000. The Laws of the time didn’t provide for a first-innings forfeit, so England’s was scored as 0/0 declared (shown as 0/0d*); South Africa’s second-innings forfeit is shown as forfeited*.
  • Successful chase: the side batting last reached its target and won.
  • Match drawn: the side was still batting when the match ended in a draw. That covers a rearguard to save the Test, a chase that ran out of time, and the odd stranger case, such as the 1939 “timeless” Test at Durban, abandoned on the tenth day with England 42 short of 696, because they had a boat home to catch.
  • Other: England’s 298/4 at The Oval in 2006, when the match was awarded to England after Pakistan refused to come back out after tea on the fourth day, in protest at a ball-tampering penalty.

Using the filters

  • Each filter is a dropdown: tick what to keep, or hover a line and click “only”.
  • The innings filter splits a 3rd innings played following on from an ordinary one.
  • The toss and result filters describe the side that made the total.
  • For 4th innings, the target is shown alongside the total.
  • The quick questions set the filters for you and open Ranked innings.

What the colours mean

  • Two boards. One for wins by runs and one for wins by an innings and runs, each from 0 upwards. Use Show to see one on its own, and View to switch between grids and wagon wheels (one ring per hundred, clockwise from the top).
  • Happened. At least one Test has been won by exactly that margin.
  • Scorigami. No Test has ever been won by that margin.

The frequency heat map shades each margin in blue by how often it has happened, from 1 up to 10 or more (darker means more often). The wicket skyscrapers don’t need it: they’re drawn to scale, from 10 wickets down to 1, so their height is the count. High-contrast colours turn the scorigamis orange.

How margins work

  • By runs: the side batting last was bowled out short of its target. The margin is how far short.
  • By an innings and runs: the winners batted only once, and their total was still bigger than both of the other side’s innings put together.
  • By wickets: the side batting last reached its target with that many wickets still standing.
  • TIE (the 0 square on the runs board): the scores finished level with the side batting last all out. It has happened twice.
  • SL, scores level (the 0 square on the innings board): a side bowled out twice for exactly the other side’s one-innings total would leave the scores level, not lost by an innings. The side batting last would still have to bat again and score 1 run to win, so “an innings and 0 runs” can never happen.
  • Draws, ties and the one awarded match (The Oval, 2006) are counted under the skyscrapers.

Using the filters

  • Team is the side that won; Against is the side that lost.
  • Toss, Follow-on and Batted also describe the winners: whether they won the toss, enforced the follow-on or won after following on, and whether they batted first.
  • Draws and ties count for either side under Team and Against, and drop out once a toss, follow-on or batting filter is on.
