Scorigami: Test Cricket (Men’s)

A scorigami is a score that has never happened before. Originally coined for the NFL, the term has since been taken up by fans of other sports. This is the Test cricket version, in two parts: the final run total of every men’s Test innings and how it ended, and the margin every men’s Test has been won by.

    How it works

    The idea

    • A scorigami is a score that has never happened. This site applies the idea to men’s Test cricket, in two halves you switch between at the top of the page:
      • Innings totals: every total from 0 up to the all-time record (), and whether any innings has finished on it.
      • Winning margins: every margin a Test has been won by: by runs, by an innings and runs, or by wickets.
    • Click or tap almost anything (a square, a slice, a bar, a row, a label) to open the full list of innings or matches behind it.

    The colours

    Normal colours

    • Green: it has happened. For innings totals, a side has been bowled out for exactly that total at least once. For margins, at least one Test has been won by exactly that margin.
    • Cream: reached, but never all out (innings totals only). Innings have finished on that total, but every one was declared, won the match by chasing, or was still batting when the match was drawn.
    • Red: a scorigami. It has never happened.
    • Dark grey: the two 0 squares (margins only). TIE holds the two tied Tests. SL, “scores level”, can never be an innings win (see How winning margins work).

    Frequency heat map

    • Turn it on under Display. Everything that has happened is shaded blue by how often: the darker, the more often. Red still means never.
    • The Grids view prints the count in each square.
    • For innings totals, a dot (or a dark outline on the rings and dial) marks totals that have never finished all out.

    High-contrast colours

    • Also under Display: turns the scorigamis orange, for anyone who finds red and green hard to tell apart.

    With filters on

    • Everything is recoloured using only the innings or matches that match your filters.

    The headline numbers

    Innings totals

    • How many totals have happened, and how many haven’t, out of every total from 0 to the record.
    • The lowest scorigami, and the most common total.
    • Latest scorigami: the most recent total to be reached for the first time.
    • Green turner: the most recent total to be bowled out on for the first time, having only ever been reached not out before.

    Winning margins

    • How many run margins and innings margins have happened, and the smallest of each that never has.
    • The most common margin, and the latest new one.
    • How many of the ten wicket margins have happened (all of them).

    Every number counts only what matches your filters.

    • The tiles at the top are buttons. Totals have happened opens the Table; totals yet to happen lists every total that has never happened; the others jump straight to that total or margin. The smaller numbers inside a tile (“then 2, 3, 4”, “before that…”) are links too.

    The views

    Grids

    • Boards of 100, one per hundred.
    • For margins, there’s one set of boards for wins by runs and one for innings wins, with the Won by wickets chart above them. Tap any of those three headings to fold that section away.

    Ranked innings and Ranked wins

    • A list, biggest or smallest first.
    • Margins are ranked one kind at a time (runs, an innings or wickets, chosen with the buttons at the top), because they aren’t on the same scale.

    Table

    • One row per total or margin, with counts and the first and latest year. Click a column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse.
    • For margins, choose runs, an innings or wickets at the top.

    Wagon wheel

    • One ring per hundred, 0–99 in the middle, each running clockwise from the top like a clock face.
    • For margins there are two wheels, runs then innings, with the wickets chart underneath.

    Dial

    • Every number in a row that you scroll through. The readout above it says what’s at the needle; tap Innings › or Matches › for the list.
    • On a computer, a strip above the dial maps the whole range, with a box showing where you are. Drag the dial or the box, scroll over it, or click it and use the arrow keys (Shift moves ten).
    • On a phone, the strip becomes a ring. Put a finger on it and slide round to the number you want. It stops at 0 and at the record. Then fine-tune with the dial.
    • For innings totals, all-out finishes are above the line and every other finish below; the all-out row stops at the highest all-out total ever (). For margins, wins by runs are above and innings wins below (on the ring: outside and inside); each row stops at its record, with the wickets chart underneath.

    The list beside the views

    • Until you pick something, the panel beside the views lists the latest scorigamis (totals or margins, depending on which half you’re in), newest first: each total or margin and the match where it first happened. For innings totals, each one has an all out (green) or not out (cream) tag beside the result, matching its square, with how it ended and the result; All out only and Not out only at the top narrow the list to one kind; on the margins side, By runs, By an innings and By wickets do the same (the two ties count as runs).
    • Pick a total or margin from the list, a headline tile, Go to or Surprise me, and the board points to it in whatever view you’re in: the square flashes on the grid, the slice pulses on the wagon wheel, the dial spins round to it, and the row lights up in the table or ranked list. It only scrolls if it isn’t already on screen, and it never changes your view. Tap one for the full story, and ‹ Latest scorigamis to come back. Wherever you are, you can always get back to it with Latest scorigamis › beside the heading above the board.

    Getting around

    • Share, at the top of the page and at the bottom, opens your device’s own share menu, on a phone or a computer. If your browser doesn’t have one, you get Copy link and Email it instead.
    • Go to jumps to a number. Surprise me picks one that has happened.
    • Show options opens the filters, time travel and quick questions; Hide options tucks them away again. They start closed, and the site remembers your choice.

    Time travel

    • Time travel sits with the filters and quick questions (on a phone, it’s the Time travel button next to them). It has two modes:
      • One year: a single handle. Drag it, use ‹ › or type a year, and everything shows only that year.
      • Range: two handles, each with its own year box and ‹ › at its own end of the bar. The left-hand arrows only move the left end, and the right-hand arrows only the right.
    • Click anywhere on the bar and the nearer handle jumps there. The arrow keys move a handle one year (Page Up and Page Down move ten).
    • ▶ plays through the years. In One year mode the year walks along. In Range mode the right-hand end moves on a year at a time, so everything builds up from wherever the left end sits (from 1877 if you leave it there). While it plays, a small player with pause and the years follows you down the page.
    • Switching from One year to Range keeps your year as the right-hand end, so “just 1948” becomes “everything up to 1948”. All years puts everything back.
    • Everything (the views, the headline numbers and the list) then counts only what happened in those years. Outlined in gold on the grids: anything that happened for the first time ever in those years. If the left end is still at the start, it’s just the last year’s new ones, marked new in the list.
    • It works with your filters, and it’s the same as the years under Era, so the two always agree.

    How innings endings are decided

    • All out: the batting side was bowled out.
      • Short-handed: the innings ended with fewer than ten wickets down because a batter was absent hurt, retired hurt or ill and didn’t come back. A few scorecards record a batter who retired hurt as “retired not out”. Those count as retired hurt here, and are marked with an asterisk (*) and a note saying where they were recorded that way.
    • Declared: the batting side closed its innings.
      • Forfeited*: counted as a declaration on 0. The only men’s Test forfeits came at Centurion in 2000. The Laws of the time didn’t provide for a first-innings forfeit, so England’s was scored as 0/0 declared (shown as 0/0d*); South Africa’s second-innings forfeit is shown as forfeited*.
    • Successful chase: the side batting last reached its target and won.
    • Match drawn: the side was still batting when the match ended in a draw. That covers a rearguard to save the Test, a chase that ran out of time, and the odd stranger case, such as the 1939 “timeless” Test at Durban, abandoned on the tenth day with England 42 short of 696, because they had a boat home to catch.
    • Other: England’s 298/4 at The Oval in 2006, when the match was awarded to England after Pakistan refused to come back out after tea on the fourth day, in protest at a ball-tampering penalty.

    How winning margins work

    • By runs: the side batting last was bowled out short of its target. The margin is how far short.
    • By an innings and runs: the winners batted only once, and their total was still bigger than both of the other side’s innings put together.
    • By wickets: the side batting last reached its target with that many wickets still standing. The wickets chart is drawn to scale, from 10 wickets down to 1.
    • TIE (0 on the runs board): the scores finished level with the side batting last all out. It has happened twice.
    • SL, scores level (0 on the innings board): a side bowled out twice for exactly the other side’s one-innings total would leave the scores level, not lost by an innings. The side batting last would still have to bat again and score 1 run to win, so “an innings and 0 runs” can never happen.
    • Draws, ties and the one awarded match (The Oval, 2006) are listed under the wickets chart.

    Filters and quick questions

    Using the filters

    • Each filter is a dropdown: tick what to keep, or hover a line and click “only”.
    • Era also takes a year range (from and to) for spans that don’t fit decades, e.g. 1975–1995.
    • Host country is where the match was played (Caribbean grounds count as West Indies, Belfast and Dublin as Ireland, Cardiff as England). Home or away is from the batting side’s point of view for innings totals, and the winner’s for margins; “neutral” means neither side’s own country, such as Pakistan’s home series in the UAE.
    • The “Showing…” line above the data lists what’s on; clear any of them with ×.
    • Teams comes first in both halves. Pick one side for all its matches (both sides’ innings, or its wins, losses, draws and ties), or two or more for only the matches between them, e.g. Australia v West Indies.
    • Pick a single side under Batting side (or Winner) and it greys out under Bowling side (or Loser), and the other way round, since no side can play itself.

    Innings totals

    • How it ended has a shortcut at the top, Not all out, that ticks or unticks every ending apart from all out in one go.
    • A total can be green and cream at once (bowled out on it once, declared on it another time), so filtering by ending shows exactly which totals have finished that way.
    • Innings splits a 3rd innings played following on from an ordinary one.
    • Toss describes the side that made the total.
    • Batting side and Bowling side narrow it to one side’s innings; Result says who won from the batting side’s point of view.
    • For 4th innings, the target is shown alongside the total.

    Winning margins

    • Winner and Loser pick sides by result, so draws and ties drop out once either is set.
    • Toss, Follow-on and Batted also describe the winners: whether they won the toss, enforced the follow-on or won after following on, and whether they batted first.
    • Draws and ties also drop out once a toss, follow-on or batting filter is on.

    Switching between the two halves

    • Teams, Era (and any year range), Host country, Home or away, Toss, and Batting side / Bowling side carry across. Batting and bowling side become Winner and Loser on the margins side, and back again; Home or away follows the batting side in one half and the winner in the other.
    • The filters that only one half has (Innings, How it ended, Result, Batted, Follow-on) stay set in their own half, ready for when you go back, but don’t apply in the other.
    • A note pops up when you switch to say what’s on, with a button to clear everything.

    Quick questions

    • They set the filters for you and show the answer as a ranked list: in Ranked innings for totals, and in the side panel for margins.
    • Change a filter afterwards and the answer updates.
