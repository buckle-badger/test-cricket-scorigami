Scorigami: Test Cricket (Men’s)

Every total a men’s Test innings has finished on, from 0 to 999

A scorigami is a score that has never happened before. The writer Jon Bois came up with it for NFL final scores, and fans have since built boards for rugby, tennis and baseball. This is the Test cricket version: the final run total of every men’s Test innings, how often each total has come up, and how those innings ended.

Tap any number to see every innings that finished on it.

What the colours mean

Each square is a team total from 0 to 999, coloured by how innings on that total have ended.

  • Complete – all out. A side has been bowled out for exactly this total at least once.
  • Complete – not out. Innings have ended on this total, but none of them all out. They were declared, won the match by chasing down a target, or were still batting when the match was drawn.
  • Scorigami. No men’s Test innings has ever finished on this total.

Under Display you can show how many times each total has happened, or switch to high-contrast colours, which turn the scorigamis orange. With filters on, the board recolours using only the innings that match.

How endings are decided

  • All out: all ten wickets down.
  • All out, short-handed: the innings ended with fewer than ten wickets down because a batter was absent hurt, retired hurt or ill and didn’t come back. A few scorecards record a batter who retired hurt as “retired not out”. Those count as retired hurt here and are marked with an asterisk (*), with a note saying where they were recorded that way.
  • Declared: the batting side closed its innings.
    • Forfeited*: counted as a declaration on 0. The only men’s Test forfeits came at Centurion in 2000. The Laws of the time didn’t provide for a first-innings forfeit, so England’s was scored as 0/0 declared (shown as 0/0d*); South Africa’s second-innings forfeit is shown as forfeited*.
  • Successful chase: the side batting last reached its target and won.
  • Match drawn: the side was still batting when the match ended in a draw. That covers a rearguard to save the Test, a chase that ran out of time, and the odd stranger case, such as the 1939 “timeless” Test at Durban, abandoned on the tenth day with England 42 short of 696, because they had a boat home to catch.
  • Other: England’s 298/4 at The Oval in 2006, when the match was awarded to England after Pakistan refused to come back out after tea on the fourth day, in protest at a ball-tampering penalty.

Using the filters

  • Each filter is a dropdown: tick what to keep, or hover a line and click “only”.
  • The innings filter splits a 3rd innings played following on from an ordinary one.
  • The toss and result filters describe the side that made the total.
  • For 4th innings, the target is shown alongside the total.
  • The quick questions set the filters for you and open Ranked innings.
